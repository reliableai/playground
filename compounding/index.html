The simulation, in five lines
  • Truth: 20 clients with true accuracy from about 56% to 83%, average 70%. Only the simulation knows it.
  • Data: synthetic requests are 6 points easier than real ones; the pilot client is one of the best clients.
  • Judge: the uncalibrated LLM judge lets 1 in 4 wrong answers pass, and fails 1 in 50 correct ones.
  • Variants: all prompt variants are equally good; we keep the one that scores best on the test set.
  • Fixing: each round fixes 30% of the misses the judge flags on the test set; only 1 in 5 of those fixes helps on new requests.

What to take away

Noise adds; biases stack, and in an evaluation most of them point up, because every choice made by looking at the score keeps the lucky errors. More test cases make the interval narrower around the wrong number. No single fix closes the gap: remove the biases (real data, a checked judge, one final measurement on untouched data), then buy precision. And even then, a new client can land anywhere in the spread between clients.
