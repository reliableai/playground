Estimating Accuracy

You ran the eval once: N examples, S of them correct. Enter the two numbers, ask a belief question, and read the answer off the Beta posterior: the curve that represents what you now believe about the true accuracy.

Uncertainty dimension: sample size · Companion to II.1

How to read and interact with this page
  1. Enter what you observed. N examples in the test set and S of them correct, or click a preset. That is all the page knows.
  2. Ask a belief question. Pick “above T”, “below T” or “between T1 and T2” and set the threshold, or click one of the three ready-made questions.
  3. Read the curve. It is the Beta posterior: your belief about the true accuracy after seeing the data. The shaded area is the answer to your question; the bar under the curve is the 95% credible interval.
  4. Grow N. Click 4 / 5, then 80 / 100, then 8,000 / 10,000, and watch the curve sharpen and the interval narrow.
  5. Change the prior. Switch to Custom Beta(8, 2) on 4 / 5 and watch the answer move; then do the same on 8,000 / 10,000 and watch it not move.

What the page computes

Bayes

Each test item is a Bernoulli trial. Start from a prior Beta(a, b); after S correct and F = N − S wrong, the posterior is Beta(a + S, b + F). The flat prior Beta(1, 1) says “before the eval, every accuracy was equally plausible”, so the posterior is Beta(S + 1, F + 1). The credible interval is the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of that curve, and any belief question is an area under it.
