Estimating Accuracy

You ran the eval once: N examples, S of them correct. Enter the two numbers and ask a belief question. Three tools answer it side by side: a Bayesian posterior, a bootstrap, and the classic 95% confidence interval. Watch the numbers converge as N grows, and notice that the sentences under them never do.

Uncertainty dimension: sample size · Companion to II.1

How to read and interact with this page
  1. Enter what you observed. N examples in the test set and S of them correct, or click a preset. That is all the page knows; every panel below is computed from these two numbers.
  2. Ask a belief question. Pick “above T”, “below T” or “between T1 and T2” and set the threshold, or click one of the three ready-made questions.
  3. Read the three panels left to right. Each shows an interval and an answer to your question. Bayes shades the area under the posterior; bootstrap counts the resamples that satisfy the question; the confidence interval cannot answer it at all, and the panel says why.
  4. Try small N, then large N. At 4 / 5 the three intervals disagree and the bootstrap is a comb. At 8,000 / 10,000 the numbers nearly coincide. The sentences under each panel are the point: they never converge.
  5. Change the prior. Switch to Custom Beta(8, 2) on 4 / 5 and watch the Bayes answer move; then do the same on 8,000 / 10,000 and watch it not move.
  6. Press the buttons. “Resample again” redraws the bootstrap; “Repeat 20 experiments” redraws the coverage picture. Both change a little every time, which is the lesson.

What each panel computes

Bayes

Each test item is a Bernoulli trial. Start from a prior Beta(a, b); after S correct and F = N − S wrong, the posterior is Beta(a + S, b + F). The flat prior Beta(1, 1) says “before the eval, every accuracy was equally plausible”, so the posterior is Beta(S + 1, F + 1). The credible interval is the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of that curve, and any belief question is an area under it.

Bootstrap

The eval produced N labels, correct or wrong. Draw N of them with replacement, compute the accuracy, repeat 10,000 times. Drawing N labels with replacement from a bag with S correct is the same as drawing Binomial(N, S/N), which is what the page does. The interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; a belief question is the share of resamples that satisfy it. The bootstrap only knows the N results it was given, so it can never propose an accuracy that the data did not already show.

95% confidence interval

The Wilson score interval, which behaves at small N and extreme p̂ where the textbook p̂ ± 1.96·SE interval does not (for 4 / 5 the textbook one runs from 45% to 115%). The coverage picture simulates 20 fresh evals of size N with true accuracy p̂, builds the interval each time, and marks the ones that miss. The 95% describes that procedure, not any single interval, and it is not a probability about the accuracy.
